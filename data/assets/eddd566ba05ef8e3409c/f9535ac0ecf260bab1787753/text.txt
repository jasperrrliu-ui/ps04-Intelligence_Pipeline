The ‘High Voltage’ Gremlin
Grace under pressure
KARIMAHOct 04, 2026Share
Seventy two hours. That’s how long it took to go from closing a group exhibition in China, to boarding a flight home, to walking straight into a live commission for the Barbican, to moving my entire studio. No decompression day, just the next thing and the next.
Alhamdulillah, I asked for all of this, prayed for most of it, and I’d take the same week again tomorrow. But I’ve learned something about what happens when the things you want arrive stacked instead of spaced out, and it’s worth being honest about rather than just posting the highlight reel.
You see, like most Gremlins, I love pressure. I like pushing the limits, not quite knowing if something is going to work until it does. The Barbican commission is the clearest example. I was asked to paint a live dance performance, in a space I’d never seen, with three hours to make and finish the work. I ended up painting almost entirely in the dark, finding the image as the dancers moved in front of me.That’s the kind of thing that makes me feel most alive as an artist.
But pressure is a strange fuel. It’s inspiring right up until the moment it starts running you instead of the other way around. The same adrenaline that gets you through a live commission on no sleep can also convince you that burnout is simply the cost of commitment. I know that what’s aligned can’t miss me, but sometimes, amidst the flurry, I still have to remind myself to stop.
Maybe the real skill isn’t learning to hold more pressure, but learning what to do before it arrives. (Preparing, pacticing etc.) So that when the deadline hits, you’re not pulling from empty, but from everything you’ve already built. And perhaps mastery is also knowing where your own edge is, and not needing to test it every single time.
I see this pattern everywhere, not just in myself but in other artists and business owners building something that depends on them specifically. It can feel deeply individual, like you’re the bottleneck. There’s a particular fear that comes with that. If you get sick, or just need a week, there’s no one to take over while you recover. The whole thing waits for you, or worse, it doesn’t wait at all. Which is why chasing mastery has to come with the skill of looking after your health too.
But what I keep coming back to is that none of this was really solo, even though it can feel that way from the inside. Ideally, the more money I make and the more people I get to work with, the more I feel part of a team, and I’d love more of that. This week was full of that. I chose to get help moving my studio. Technically, I could have done it alone, but I was exhausted, and thank God for the movers who came and helped me pack everything up. In China, there were curators and a whole team holding the group show together behind the scenes. At the Barbican, one member of the team simply showing me around the space with a friendly face felt like a small grounding grace in the middle of everything. I didn’t carry this week by myself at all.
What got me through was smaller than any of this; writing down what I’m grateful for, daily exercise and a few systems I lean on to stay on top of work and life admin.
Grace, when you’re depleted, isn’t a feeling that shows up and carries you. It’s closer to a muscle you keep firing in short bursts, one load of washing at a time.
And so, to bring this home, what’s the lesson when the pressure is on? I don’t think the answer is less pressure. Inshallah there are so many dreams and things I still want to make, so less pressure is never really the goal. I think it’s learning to use these high voltage moments as bursts, then building in time to recharge and bounce back. Finding centre fast, and riding the momentum.
