The Addams Family at Studio Tenn
Freshly Kooky and Spooky!
Vivian Acevedo  |
October 15, 2026
StudioTenn’s production of “The Addam’s Family” is perfectly kooky and spooky with some new twists that allow this household story and characters to remain exciting! I attended the show Friday (9-9) and was thoroughly entertained the entire time. “The Addam’s Family” musical adds on to the already-established dysfunctional, yet loving family and furthers the story rather than recreating movie plots. In this adaptation, Morticia (Sandra Allen) and Gomez (Brian Michael Jones) are navigating raising their teenager into adulthood as Wednesday (Anna Jones) falls in love with a boy, Lucas (Dylan James), whose family is the stark contrast to the Addam’s with their colorful clothes and suburban ways. Thus, triggering silly and spooky events that encapsulate the Addams’ uniqueness.(feature photo Wednesday played by Anna Jones photo:Sammy Hearn)
Lurch (Scott Patrick Wilson), Morticia (Sandra Allen) and Gomez (Brian Michael Jones). (photo: Sammy Hearn)
This new plotline is very fun, however, it provides a new challenge for the actors and actresses. They are required to expand on the story, while simultaneously keeping the caricatures they play as recognizable to the watchers as possible. The audience expects eccentric and hilarious Gomez, elegant and gothic Morticia, woeful and impassive Wednesday, and a goofy Uncle Fester. How do we balance accuracy with originality? The show itself has fantastic moments that highlight these beloved characters, but the StudioTenn ensemble created an original and fresh adaptation. Every person on stage played their characters with incredible understanding, it felt as if they were one. I have seen many productions of “The Addam’s Family,” but this production proves why it is necessary to keep producing. There is always something new and special that can be discovered from a cult-classic.
The costumes stood out immediately from the very opening scene and I would love to applaud the costume designers and team. I am beyond impressed by the originality and intentionality of every character’s individual wardrobe. To keep on the same point, they allowed the characters to be recognizable as caricatures, but did not copy other “Addam’s Family” productions in their diversity. Every little detail was so exciting to notice! For example: Morticia being in a sparkly low cut dress kept her elegance and eccentricism but still strayed from the classic black dress with bell sleeves, Wednesday kept the classic bob but had a wolf cut and micro bangs which gave her incredible angst and reminded us she is becoming her own person, Alice was in bright yellow but her red wig, red nails, and lipstick aided her fiery personality! I gasped when the ancestors came out from the grave in their all-white, sparkly, ensembles that provided each a character story. This excellent artistry can further the value of a show so incredibly well and give the actors and actors wearing them the confidence in their ability to portray these characters. I am so excited to follow the artistic career of these designers and hope to be in the audience for their next venture!
Gomez, Lucas (Dylan James), Wednesday
It is hard to not write endless paragraphs describing these actors and actresses’ performances and the brilliance in them! From top to bottom, this cast gave this show their all and it was evident in the electricity in the room. Brian Michael Jones delivered a hilarious Gomez. It was his character’s responsibility to deliver every crowd pleasing line and silly joke and it was done seamlessly. Jones balanced incredible vocals with difficult lines and monologues excellently and kept the pace of the show flowing. Sandra Allen was a beautiful Morticia, providing melancholy hilarious line readings with show-stopping numbers to counteract which kept her character extremely engaging. Her performance of “Death Is Just Around the Corner” was hilarious and skillfully performed. Anna Jones’ vocals as Wednesday were (ironically) killer. Her act one solo “Pulled” blew the roof of the theater vocally and demonstrated her stellar acting chops as Wednesday grappled with feeling a new and happy emotion, love! Not to mention, Jones maintained Wednesday’s signature grim facials the majority of the show, making every smile extra meaningful.
The two performances that stood out to me in particular were Jacob Waid’s Uncle Fester and Megan Murphy Chamber’s Alice Beineke. Waid stole the show the moment he stepped on stage! From dancing on the graves of the Addams’ ancestors to professing his love to the moon, Uncle Fester is a loveable and comical character. Waid gave a masterclass in stage presence and physical comedy as he narrated the story and led ensemble numbers. On top of all of this, he hit some incredible notes! I am still in awe of Megan Murphy Chamber’s portrayal of Alice, a deceptively difficult character to play! The build-up to “Waiting” in which the audience continues to receive clues of Alice’s secret until she completely explodes is what makes the song a showstopper. It was indeed the highlight of the show. Watching this character completely unravel after exclusively seeing her uptight and reserved, takes some very nuanced acting. Chambers went for it, she delivered a hilarious performance that had the audience howling in laughter. Her vocals were impeccable and her acting choices were bold! What more can you ask for in a performance! Brava!
Studio Tenn production of “The Addam’s Family” is a must-do Halloween activity! Whether you are a long-time Addams Family fan or just love live theater, this show has something for everyone! The professionalism and value of this theater is always astounding, this cast and crew did not disappoint. The show runs through October 25th, get your tickets now here
