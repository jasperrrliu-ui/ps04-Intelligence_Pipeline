The Gremlin with a New Studio
The room you grow into
KARIMAHOct 11, 202651Share
Hello my most abundant, beautiful, naughty little gremlins.
I am dying to write to you, and unlike my usual planned self, I’m writing this one straight from the hip on a Sunday with no overthinking. We are just about stepping into week 42, which means there are ten weeks left of the year. Hm.
They say, and by ‘they’ I mean the popular voices out there, that we overestimate what we can do in a year and underestimate what we can do in five. I’ve been moving with that because this year I shot for a few things and didn’t get them. But the misses didn’t totally miss, they just primed me to try again next year. And so I’m confident, inshallah, that they’ll arrive within five. They just weren’t meant for this year, and I’m learning to let that be true without making it mean anything bigger. Maybe there are things that haven’t landed for you yet this year either. Give them five. If you still want them by then, I have no doubt they’ll be there.
Alongside the misses there have been personal wins and personal tests, and one of the biggest happened this week. I stepped into a new studio.
For about a year and a half I’d been working from a studio that was so typically London. It was a makeshift space, a good working space in many ways, but with no natural light, low ceilings and the occasional visit from a few mice and stragglers it wasn’t the dream. For a night owl, or for someone who isn’t affected by light, it would have been fine. For me it was the wrong match, environmentally and architecturally, and the drawbacks kept adding up.
“A woman must have money and a room of her own if she is to write fiction.”  - Virginia Woolf
To be honest, it was only when I was in Morocco this year that I really saw it. The amount I was making, and how happy it made me to be working in light, showed me that what I’d filed under a nice to have was actually a non-negotiable. The thing is, studios and rent in London are expensive, and I know it sounds crazy, but natural light is treated as a luxury, especially for art studios, which is so counterproductive. It took being somewhere else for me to see how much my environment feeds my inspiration. It’s a hard line to walk, because before that I was just pushing through the winter, and I know a big part of this practice is not making excuses and simply showing up. But when I walked into the new space, I jumped around and danced and felt so happy, and I knew; “no, this really is important”.
Obviously I’ve painted in lots of makeshift spaces, from China to theatres to all sorts of different locations, so I’m used to working with a few things, making the best of it and keeping it moving. Making on the move strips the practice back to what you actually need, and it shows me which habits are about the work and which are just about the room. I’ll always be able to pivot, but having more stable roots, is more than a nice to have.
So when I look at these last ten weeks, I don’t see a year that fell short, I see a year that rearranged the foundations. A studio filled with light was once on my vision board, and now I’m standing in it. It might sound like a quiet dream next to the big shiny ones, but it changes what’s possible in the next five years more than most of them would. And who knows where stronger foundations can take a little ragamuffin Gremlin? I think it matters far more than I can even see right now.
So here’s to the final ten weeks. Less chasing, more creating and more light, quite literally.
