Everything you need to know

Frieze Week | London14 to 18 October 2026 . Frieze London and Frieze Masters from £38 . Most of the art spots around the city are free.

The gist: for five days in October the art world migrates to London, and every gallery, museum and satellite fair in the city times its best opening to land in the same week. Most of it is free. So I made you this guide →
Hey my beautiful Museum Rats. This week is a round-up, because there is so much going on for Frieze it’s almost impossible to pick one thing for this little rat to see. Plus, I want you to have this guide ahead of time.
First, what is Frieze? Frieze is an art fair where elite galleries rent a booth in a temporary tent in Regent’s Park and sell work for five days, mostly to collectors and institutions rather than the average Joe. There are two tents: Frieze London holds contemporary art, and Frieze Masters sits a short walk across the park with everything made before 2000. Tickets start at £38 and you have to buy them online. Once you leave there is no re-entry, so go in knowing which tent you want.
Frieze Week is then the wider cultural event, where museums and institutions tag onto the fact that a lot of buyers, artists and art people are in town. Cork Street and Dover Street put up the shows they’ve been saving, museums open the exhibitions they want reviewed, and a handful of smaller fairs take over hotels and hospitality spaces. The good thing is that a lot of these extra activities are free, and it’s a great week to do some museum hopping.

If you only have one day
Start at 11AM in Regent’s Park with Frieze Sculpture, which is free. It’s outdoors and never busy before lunch. Walk south into Fitzrovia for Minor Attractions in the hotel bedrooms, (also free) and more accessable/fun than the tent. Cut west into Mayfair and do Cork Street and Dover Street on foot, where every gallery is free and every one of them has its best show of the year up. Finish at the Barbican for the Curve, which costs nothing, and the Ryman if you still have the legs. Total spend, a travelcard.
Museum Rat’s verdict?
Don’t mistake the tent for the whole event. The fair is a trade show, and a fascinating one at that, but if you’re simply after bursts of great art, the real event is Frieze Week itself. And much of the week is free. Cork Street on a Thursday afternoon with a coffee can be a better day than Regent’s Park on a Saturday with forty pounds gone and no re-entry.
If you do go to the fair, go Thursday at one when it opens. Pick one tent, not both, and leave while you still like it.
Planning Your Visit
What: Frieze London and Frieze Masters, 14 to 18 October 2026, The Regent’s Park, NW1.Tickets: from £38 for a single fair, from £76 combined, £32 combined for students. Online only. No re-entry once you leave.Hours: Thursday from 1pm, Friday and Saturday 11 to 7, Sunday 11 to 6.Free all week: Frieze Sculpture, Minor Attractions, the Barbican Curve, and every commercial gallery in Mayfair and the East.Worth knowing: Frieze Sculpture stays up until 1 November, so if the week is too much, go the week after when the park is empty.
