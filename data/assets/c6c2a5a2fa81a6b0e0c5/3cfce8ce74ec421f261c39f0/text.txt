Interview: Boulder Ensemble Theater Company
Ari Lubin
September 26, 2026
In this interview, Rodger Hara speaks with director and Boulder Ensemble Theater Company (BETC) ensemble member Candace Orino and actor Michael Morgan about The Weir, Conor McPherson’s acclaimed play set in a rural pub in northwestern Ireland.
Orino and Morgan discuss the play’s themes of storytelling, community, and connection; the process of casting a company of strong storytellers; and the challenges of capturing the distinct accents and rhythms of rural Ireland. Orino also describes the production’s scenic design, dramaturgical preparation, and transition from the Savoy in Denver to BETC’s Boulder space.
The Weir opens at BETC in Boulder on October 9, following its Denver run at the Savoy. (Air Date 9/23/26).
Listen to the interview here:
Transcript:
Rodger Hara: I’ve got Candace and Michael on the line. Welcome to KGNU and Boulder. I’ve invited you to be with me to chat about a play that is going to open soon at BETC in Boulder. Candace and Michael, good morning.
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: Good morning.
Rodger Hara: So please introduce yourselves.
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: My name is Candace Orino, and I’m an ensemble member for Boulder Ensemble Theatre Company. I have the pleasure of directing our play.
I’m Michael Morgan, and I’m playing Jack in The Weir.
Rodger Hara: Talk about The Weir. Why was that particular play chosen for this season?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: The Weir is one that BETC started talking about a few years ago. I think that every season when you’re choosing your plays, it’s really about finding the right mix to create this special alchemy of storytelling. This year was the year, after this one had been floated for a while, that the alchemy felt right.
It’s a beautiful play. It’s survived and continues to be produced several decades on from when it was originally written because it still has so much to say that people really connect with. So it was probably only a matter of time before that story needed to be told here in the Front Range.
Rodger Hara: Michael, what does the play say?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: Well, it’s full of history and story, and says a lot of things about how the stories that they tell—the Weir is about telling stories—and how these stories connect us and what they reveal about us.
The stories that we tell in The Weir start very spookily, but they end up being very revealing about each person telling those stories, and kind of revealing about these people in general, the town that they live in, and the world that they live in.
It’s just a wonderfully universal idea. I have been several times in my life in gatherings very similar to where we tell stories in a very similar way. So it’s a very common feeling, and it’s just been really, really lovely to work on.
Rodger Hara: Candace, can you talk about the background of the play? Who is the playwright, the setting of the show, and who else is in the cast? Are there names of people that the Boulder listening audience in particular would recognize from previous BETC performances?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: Sure. The play, The Weir, was written by Conor McPherson in the ’90s. The play takes place in a small rural pub in northwestern Ireland. It’s a dark and windy night.
In this local pub, usually you just find the same cast of characters: a few guys who come for a drink at the end of a long day, and they’re in community together. But on this night, things are shaken up a little bit because a woman has moved to this tiny town from the big city of Dublin. They don’t quite know what to expect about the fact that she’s made that move and that she’s going to come to the pub tonight.
So that’s the launching point of the play. Then, as Michael said, as they gather together, they end up telling stories to one another, and community forms as a result of that.
We do have a fabulous cast of actors. We have Michael here, who is playing Jack, who’s like the patriarch in this room. I say that in the best way. He just is the good-sense patriarch who holds the community together in so many ways.
There’s the young shop owner, and he’s played by Ryan Stack, who Boulder audiences will know from BETC and also from the Colorado Shakespeare Festival.
We have Colin Martin. This is his first production at BETC. Colin is a California transplant and a very welcome talent to our community.
Mark Collins plays Jim, and audiences will remember, I think his most recent production at BETC was Enemy of the People. But he’s known all throughout Denver, just as Michael is.
And then Colleen Lea plays Valerie, and Colleen was recently seen in Mary Jane at Boulder Ensemble this past spring.
Rodger Hara: How did you go about casting the show?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: BETC holds an open casting that then gets whittled down to callbacks. They put all the season directors in a room at the same time, and we see actors read for all of the plays of the season.
It’s really an embarrassment of riches of talent. So inevitably you end up needing to do a second callback, or even sometimes more than that, because there are just so many people and it’s hard to choose.
But I knew I needed really good storytellers because this play does center on stories. That was the thing that I was looking for, and I’m very fortunate with the group that I was able to claim from all the other directors. They’re a great bunch.
Rodger Hara: With the setting of the play in northwestern Ireland, in a small town, Michael, what kind of challenges has that presented to you with regard to the accent? Have you had to use an Irish accent in other shows?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: Yeah, it’s been a while. I do remember having an Irish accent in a show about 20 years ago that I can’t even tell you the title of because it’s been so long. But it was just kind of your general Dublin-ish accent. We didn’t have a vocal coach back then.
We are very fortunate this time to have Jeffrey Parker, who is an amazing vocal coach and works all around the region. He’s currently working on, I don’t know, Candace, two or three other projects right now?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: Yeah. He’s in high demand.
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: He’s in high demand, and he’s just wonderful. The most important thing is that we all sound like we’re from the same place. Even if we have little details that we miss along the way, it’s important that we all sound kind of similar, except, of course, for the Valerie character, who is from Dublin. She’s slightly different.
Rural Ireland is like anywhere else in the world, kind of like our South, where you’ll go across the street and the accent will be a little different. You’ll go to the next town and the accent will be a little different.
So we’ve been zeroing in on little subtle changes that happen from region to region. It’s been a lot of fun, actually. It’s very challenging, but it’s mostly muscle memory. It’s all just kind of remembering how your mouth and your tongue work precisely to get those sounds just right.
It’s been very challenging, but a lot of fun.
Rodger Hara: Candace, who did your scenic design, and what are the challenges of moving the scenery from the Savoy to Boulder?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: Tina Anderson is our scenic designer. She’s also an ensemble member at BETC, so she’s done a lot of the beautiful things that you’ve seen on BETC stages. She’s got this shift from Denver to Boulder down now. She’s really good at it.
She designs knowing the needs of both spaces ahead of time and ensures that the design will work for both spaces. So she’s quite good at making it a fluid move.
But it certainly asks a lot of our crew because they have to hang lights in two spaces and focus lights and tech them in two spaces and account for how the sound will be different in both spaces.
So it’s not just the set; it’s all of the elements of design. We will go into a tech rehearsal process when we move to Boulder, in particular to give our technical team what they need to ensure a seamless transition and the same storytelling experience from one space to the next.
Rodger Hara: How have you prepared the cast for the play in terms of dramaturgy?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: Dramaturgy is definitely a part of my process as a director. I will spend a long time reading, researching, and connecting things that are found in the text to resources that help our cast and our designers get a deeper insight into the world of the play.
I always make a dramaturgy packet that has descriptions and photographs and vocabulary guides. The intention certainly is that it helps us all launch feeling really confident about the world of the play.
Whether it’s a play that takes place in another state in the U.S. or certainly one like this that’s in another country, it can be so helpful to have something like that.
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: Roger, I’ll tell you, having a good dramaturgy packet is such a wonderful gift for an actor to be able to have even before we sit down to read the play for the first time together. The one Candace put together was wonderful. It gave us so much detail and so many things.
I’m a researcher myself, and I love sitting down and finding those things, but having all of that right at your fingertips was so vital, and it was so great. Great stuff.
Rodger Hara: Glad to hear that.
So another show will open in early September, mid-September, at the Savoy in Denver, and this particular program won’t air until the 23rd of September. So when will the show open in Boulder?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: That is a great question. Let me just look at my calendar here. The Boulder opening is October 9th.
So since this program is airing on the 23rd, there will still be two weeks of the program in Denver. You can check the website to see if tickets are still available, or you can plan to make a night of it and enjoy some time in Boulder starting October 9th.
Rodger Hara: What is the website address for people to go to to look up dates and ticket availability?
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: That’s at betc.org, B-E-T-C.org.
Rodger Hara: B-E-T-C.org. Go there to find information about ticket availability to see The Weir at the Savoy in Denver, and then at BETC in Boulder.
So Candace and Michael, thank you very, very much for your time. I, for one, very much look forward to seeing the show and getting to enjoy hearing the accents and getting transported across the sea to Ireland.
Boulder Ensemble Theater Company: Thank you, Roger. Okay. Can’t wait to see you there, Roger. Thank you.
Ari Lubin
