# How a Stage Takes Shape: Editorial Policy v2

The audience includes theatre fans without professional stage-design training. Each edition asks how recent creators turn stories into playable spaces and lighting, what beginners can learn, and where AI-assisted design tools may reasonably help. Focus on scenic design, lighting, audience attention, rehearsal, and iteration. Include sound, costume, or stage management only when it explains collaboration or product boundaries. Exclude equipment specifications, show advertising, and unrelated home or fashion coverage.

1. **Freshness.** Use original materials published from 29 days before the run's `as_of_date` through that date. Unknown dates, reposted old recordings, and old stories with changed dates do not establish new developments. When a recent interview discusses an older production, identify what is recent: the interview, analysis, or research.
2. **Relevance and substance.** Accept full-text or timestamped audio/video material that explains a design choice, process, constraint, audience experience, or AI capability. A title, short announcement, repost, or generic promotion is not substantive evidence.
3. **Claim-level credibility.** A designer interview supports what that designer says about their process, not automatically a universal rule. Label commercial interests in brand interviews and product pieces. Identify preprints as unreviewed and describe their sample and evaluation limits. Audience comments can indicate experience but cannot replace independent sources.
4. **Novelty and perspectives.** Prefer a range of professional designers, collaborators, beginners or audience members, and researchers. A repost or podcast and its YouTube mirror count as one underlying item. Several outlets repeating one announcement do not independently confirm it.
5. **Uncertainty and disagreement.** Separate direct facts, source claims, and project inferences. Explain differences in context or constraints when evidence conflicts; do not manufacture a debate. State when drawings, direct observation, or verified audio are unavailable.
6. **Audio/video evidence.** Claims about media must point to an exact transcript segment and timestamp. Check names, terminology, and consequential quotes from automatic Whisper transcripts against the recording before publication. A title is not a sufficient evidence locator.
7. **Decisions.** For each date-eligible item, return `accept`, `reject`, or `defer` with a reason. Defer when full text, date, or identity is uncertain, or when media passages need review. Acceptance does not guarantee that an item will be cited; a human still reviews and approves the final edition.
8. **Match the material type.** Web pages and RSS articles are not audio/video transcripts; do not defer a full-text article because it lacks `transcription_verified`. A promotional creator interview may support a specific attributed account of process, with its commercial purpose and lack of independent verification stated. YouTube captions and podcast ASR may be candidate evidence, but key names, terms, and quotes need audio verification before publication. Selection reasons must agree with the collected text, media type, and mechanical date result.

The three connected pieces answer: (01) Which recent professional-design practices or findings matter to beginners? (02) Which common claims about design and AI withstand cross-source scrutiny, and which remain uncertain? (03) How can these findings shape a beginner learning scaffold and the boundaries of a future app? Each piece uses at least three independent publishers and includes a cross-source inference and a limitation or counterpoint.
